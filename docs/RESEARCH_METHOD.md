# How the research was gathered

This page answers the first marker comment in full: what was
collected, from where, over which period, and how much weight each
claim carries.

## Method in one paragraph

Secondary-source analysis. The group studied four South African
incidents from January to May 2026 through regulator guidance, the
POPIA text, national press coverage and peer-reviewed background
reading. Nobody was interviewed. No live systems were touched. The
register this page describes holds the cases, and every row points
at its source.

## The four cases

Gauteng Provincial Government, Statistics South Africa, Standard
Bank and Liberty Group. Summaries sit in the research_cases table
with medium confidence: the incidents are well reported, while exact
figures vary between outlets.

## Sources and confidence

Eleven sources are seeded from the group's own Deliverable 2 and
Deliverable 3 bibliographies. Each carries a confidence label. High
means two independent reports agree, such as the Stats SA breach
covered by both ITWeb and the regulator discussion. Medium means one
solid report, such as the IOL piece on Liberty. Low means background
reading that frames the analysis without proving a finding.

Field notes attached to sources are sealed envelopes, opened only
with the research key. The seal proves tampering, not truth: a
sealed note is unchanged, and its confidence label still decides how
far to trust it.

## What the method cannot do

Press coverage follows what is visible. Internal timelines, exact
affected counts and unreported incidents stay outside this method.
The software fixtures respect that limit by using invented
organisations, so no demo row can be mistaken for a research claim.
